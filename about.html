    About Garmin Fenix 9

    Liam July 31, 2026

    Upgrades to Look Forward To

    1. New Elevate 6 Optical Heart Rate Sensor

    Garmin hasn't updated its optical heart rate sensor in a long time. The Elevate 6 is said to improve accuracy. It may also bring new health features like sweat/pressure monitoring and background irregular heartbeat detection.

    2. 5G Redcap + Satellite Messaging

    The Fenix 8 Pro already has LTE and satellite features. But they made the watch thicker and shortened battery life. The Fenix 9 will focus on making these technologies better – thinner, more power-efficient, and more stable. Also, 5G Redcap (a lighter version of 5G) might appear on a watch for the first time.

    3. A More Mature MicroLED Screen

    The MicroLED version of the Fenix 8 Pro was impressive, but Garmin admitted it also made the watch thicker and drained the battery faster. The Fenix 9 may bring a second-generation MicroLED that actually delivers on the promise of saving power. I used a Fenix 8 Pro for two days back in October 2025, and I wasn't that impressed. It didn't feel like a big upgrade – it just seemed to use more battery.

    4. A Stronger Processor

    The Fenix series has used the same processor platform for three generations now (the frx55 Fenix 7 in 2022, the fr65 and Fenix 7P in 2023, the Fenix 8 in 2024, and the fr70 in 2025). It's very likely that the Fenix 9 will get a new chip. Hopefully, that means smoother UI response and better energy efficiency. This could be the biggest highlight of the upgrade.


    5. 43mm Size Gets Premium Features

    On the Fenix 8 Pro, the LTE and satellite features didn't come to the smaller 43mm version. The Fenix 9 might finally give small-wrist users all the flagship functions too.

    What Users Really Want

    Looking around online, Fenix users actually have pretty simple wishes:

    - Thinner body (the Fenix 8 Pro is really too thick)
    - Bigger screen-to-body ratio
    - Fewer bugs (you know what I mean)
    - Smarter health and GPS tools

    Most users don't need Garmin to do anything crazy. They just want the company to fix what needs fixing and optimize what needs optimizing.
